# Architectural graphic taxonomy

Choose the family from the question being answered, not from a fashionable visual style. A coordinated set may combine several families, but each image should carry one primary claim.

## Representation selector

| Architectural question | Preferred representation | Why it works | Common misuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where is it and what surrounds it? | location map, context plan, figure-ground | shows territory, urban grain, edges, and relationships | decorative map with no design implication |
| How do people or vehicles arrive and move? | plan, section, path overlay, sequence | makes entry, route, threshold, and vertical movement explicit | arrows floating over an unreadable render |
| What is the organizing idea? | parti in plan or section | reduces the project to axes, hierarchy, boundary, solid/void, or a few dominant relations | abstract icon unrelated to actual organization |
| How does form change? | repeated-base massing sequence | isolates each transformation and preserves comparison | every step changes view, crop, and base geometry |
| How are uses distributed or related? | plan, stacked axonometric, adjacency/bubble diagram | shows zoning, adjacency, overlap, shared cores, and separation | rainbow program colors without spatial hierarchy |
| How do levels, light, terrain, gravity, or views relate? | technical section | expresses vertical and perceptual relations directly | drone perspective standing in for a section |
| How is the facade or roof organized? | elevation, unfolded surface, module/seam diagram, axonometric fragment | reveals datum, rhythm, openings, modules, layers, and edge conditions | material render used as proof of construction |
| How is it assembled? | exploded axonometric, cutaway, detail fragment, step sequence | reveals order, interfaces, and dependencies | exploded advertisement filled with invented fasteners |
| What changes between existing and proposal? | same-view, same-outline, same-scale A/B | makes the design move falsifiable and immediately visible | only color or captions change while geometry barely changes |
| What changes over time? | phasing, demolition/reuse map, construction or operation sequence | reuses one base and isolates temporal states | unrelated scenes masquerading as a sequence |

## Diagram and non-render image families

### 1. Site and context

Possible content:

- location at region, city, district, and immediate-site scales;
- figure-ground, block structure, plot edges, setbacks, and interfaces;
- topography, hydrology, vegetation, orientation, and open-space networks;
- pedestrian, cycle, transit, service, emergency, and vehicle access;
- sun, wind, shade, noise, views, landmarks, and sensory observations;
- positive/negative influences, opportunities, constraints, and design response.

Use measured contours, climate data, or regulatory boundaries only when sourced. Separate observed site conditions from predicted or proposed responses.

### 2. Parti, geometry, and spatial organization

Possible content:

- primary axes, datums, centers, grids, symmetry, and asymmetry;
- boundaries, thresholds, enclosures, courts, and voids;
- hierarchy, position, alignment, scale, and solid/void;
- dominant plan or sectional organization;
- a small number of spatial relationships that explain the whole.

Omit wall thickness, furniture, and minor articulation when they do not support the claim. A parti is not a miniature fully detailed plan.

### 3. Massing and form transformation

Possible content:

- site envelope, subtraction, addition, split, lift, step, rotate, carve, bridge, or open;
- response to street, view, daylight, wind, access, or program;
- base mass to final form in a short sequence;
- option comparison using one camera and one base.

Use one transformation per frame. Keep the ground plane, viewpoint, scale, material language, and context stable.

### 4. Program, adjacency, and zoning

Possible content:

- public/private, served/servant, noisy/quiet, wet/dry, front/back-of-house;
- program clusters, shared cores, support zones, and outdoor rooms;
- vertical stacking, adjacency, overlap, and controlled separation;
- occupancy or schedule patterns when backed by data.

Use a limited semantic palette. Color should identify program groups, not decorate every room independently.

### 5. Circulation, sequence, and experience

Possible content:

- approach, entry, threshold, transition, pause, destination, and exit;
- horizontal and vertical circulation;
- separate user, service, logistics, accessibility, and emergency routes;
- spatial sequence, enclosure, light, and framed views;
- desire lines and conflict points.

Use plan for route logic, section for level changes, and axonometric only when movement crosses several levels or systems.

### 6. Environmental and performance logic

Possible content:

- solar exposure, shade, daylight, glare, and seasonal change;
- wind, ventilation paths, pressure zones, and sheltered areas;
- water collection, flow, detention, discharge, reuse, and planted systems;
- thermal zoning, buffer spaces, envelope response, and energy flows;
- acoustic, comfort, or environmental gradients.

Distinguish observed, simulated, calculated, generic, and proposed information. Do not invent arrows, endpoints, or performance values because they look plausible.

### 7. Structure and load path

Possible content:

- structural grid and primary/secondary systems;
- span direction, support hierarchy, bracing, core, and stability concept;
- conceptual gravity and lateral load paths;
- transfer conditions and structural zones;
- existing/reinforced/new structure in adaptive reuse.

Use load arrows or capacities only when supported. A conceptual load path is not an engineered calculation.

### 8. Tectonic, assembly, and detail

Possible content:

- means and order of assembly;
- layer families and their interfaces;
- local attachment, bearing, overlap, drainage, ventilation, tolerance, and access concepts;
- fragment proportions and relationship of parts to whole;
- manufacturer-family logic as a generic reference.

Use exact fasteners, profiles, hidden layers, waterproofing, dimensions, fire barriers, or tolerances only when documented. Otherwise abstract the interface and mark the unresolved zone.

### 9. Envelope, facade, roof, material, and module

Possible content:

- datum, bay, opening, panel, seam, strip, rib, joint, and field relationships;
- facade depth, shading, transparency, opacity, and material transitions;
- roof continuity, edge, ridge, valley, module, seam, and attached layer;
- material palette or finish logic as a secondary explanatory image;
- fragment-to-whole relationship.

Keep material impression separate from construction proof. Use elevation for rhythm and a fragment for interface logic.

### 10. Existing condition, intervention, and adaptive reuse

Possible content:

- retain, repair, remove, add, reinforce, open, close, and reprogram;
- historical layers and current condition;
- demolition/reuse maps;
- existing/proposed spatial or facade comparison;
- reversible and irreversible interventions.

Use a common base. Make status legible by geometry, opacity, hatch, or a small semantic palette rather than by captions alone.

### 11. Construction, phasing, operation, and time

Possible content:

- fabrication, delivery, lifting, assembly, enclosure, and finishing order;
- temporary supports and access zones when verified;
- occupation, conversion, maintenance, seasonal, or event scenarios;
- short/medium/long-term phases;
- dependencies and decision gates.

Reuse one base view. Show only the elements relevant to each step and ghost completed/contextual work.

### 12. Same-scale comparisons and options

Possible content:

- existing/proposed;
- option A/B/C;
- fragmented/aggregated;
- open/closed;
- dry/wet season;
- before/during/after.

Lock projection, crop, outline, scale, line weight, background, lighting, and semantic colors. Change the geometry or relation that embodies the design decision; a mere recolor is not a meaningful architectural comparison.

### 13. Schematic projection drawings and board bases

Possible content:

- simplified plan, section, elevation, roof plan, or axonometric base;
- diagrammatic technical section;
- contextual ghosted model with one highlighted system;
- clean line-and-fill background image for later annotation;
- coordinated family of non-photorealistic views.

State `schematic and not dimensionally authoritative` unless exact source geometry is available. Add dimensions, scale bars, north arrows, legends, and formal notation later in a deterministic drawing or layout tool.

### 14. Perceptual and atmosphere analysis

Possible content:

- enclosure, compression/release, sequence, view framing, light gradient, and surface character;
- diagrammatic silhouettes or restrained material studies;
- a technical/perceptual image distinct from a photorealistic effect render.

Use this family only when it explains a spatial condition. If the primary goal is mood, marketing, or a convincing inhabited scene, route to the effect-image workflow instead.

## Building a coordinated set

For a multi-image board, prefer a small grammar:

- one shared base geometry per project or comparison family;
- one projection per question;
- one stable semantic palette;
- one accent per analytical layer;
- one claim per image;
- one visual hierarchy across all images;
- one later annotation system outside the generated raster.

Common coherent sets include:

- context → parti → massing → program → circulation;
- evidence → abstraction → principle → proposal;
- existing → issue → intervention → result;
- whole → system → assembly → detail;
- plan organization → sectional consequence → facade or roof response;
- observed environmental condition → analytical model → design response.

## Method research basis

The taxonomy synthesizes durable methods found in architecture teaching and practice rather than copying any single visual:

- [Analyzing Architecture through Diagrams — CCC Architecture](https://www.cccarchitecture.org/analyzing-architecture-through-diagrams)
- [Architecture Diagrams Master Guide — First In Architecture](https://www.firstinarchitecture.co.uk/architecture-diagrams/)
- [Site Analysis — First In Architecture](https://www.firstinarchitecture.co.uk/architecture-site-analysis-guide/)
- [Exploded Axonometric — Columbia GSAPP](https://www.arch.columbia.edu/books/reader/503-exploded-axonometric)
- [Core Studio I — Rice Architecture](https://arch.rice.edu/latest/issue-95/core-studio-i)
- [Research and Analysis — M3H Architecten](https://m3h.nl/en/expertise/research-and-analysis/)
